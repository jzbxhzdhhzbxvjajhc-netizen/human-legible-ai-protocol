## The original problem definition

AI capability is advancing faster than the language bridge ordinary people need in order to use it. The first barrier is often not model intelligence, access, or willingness. It is the sentence that makes a person think:

> “This sounds technical. Maybe I am not qualified to ask.”

This project begins from a different assumption: a person may use a phone and computer normally while knowing none of the software industry's vocabulary. They may not know to ask for a “project,” “workflow,” “API,” “database,” “deployment,” “agent,” or “audit.” They may only know that something in daily life is repetitive, confusing, expensive, or frustrating.

The burden of crossing that gap belongs to the AI and its makers—not to the user.

## The protocol claims fixed by v0.1.1

1. **AI capability must first be translated into a real-life problem it can reduce.** Do not begin with a catalogue of technical features.
2. **AI execution must be translated back into a human decision.** The user should understand what is about to happen, why it matters, what could go wrong, and whether they need to approve it.
3. **The protocol is bidirectional.** Everyday language becomes an executable task; technical process becomes an understandable choice.
4. **The ordinary person starts with zero industry vocabulary.** Normal phone or computer use is not the same as knowing software concepts.
5. **The best translation may remove a term instead of defining it.** “Create a project” may simply become “give this task its own place so the files do not get mixed up.”
6. **Every new unexplained term creates cognitive debt.** An explanation that creates several new “what does that mean?” questions has failed.
7. **Understanding is not enough; control is the test.** A successful explanation helps the person make the next decision without hiding consequences or risk.
8. **Plain language must not falsify the technical reality.** Simplicity is not permission to omit a fact that could change the decision.
9. **Basic questions are legitimate.** A product label, button name, or familiar-looking interface is not an explanation.
10. **The protocol must be testable.** START, CONTROL, and EXPLAIN behavior is supported by examples, evaluation cases, bilingual decision parity, and a reference Skill.

## What this record does—and does not—claim

This public record documents when the project assembled these claims into a named, versioned, testable protocol with a reference implementation. It helps future readers compare dates, definitions, and lineage.

It does not claim ownership of every future phrase such as “AI should speak plainly,” “AI understands me,” or similar independently developed ideas. The project's defensible identity is the specific protocol, its published wording and tests, its canonical history, and the official project that maintains conformance.

Published versions are not silently rewritten. Corrections that change meaning receive a new version so the public timeline remains inspectable.

## 中文原始主张

我们定义的“普通人”，不是“刚入门的程序员”，而是会正常使用手机和电脑、但不知道软件行业术语的人。他可能连“项目”是什么都不知道，也不知道自己该问 API、数据库或自动化；他只知道生活里有件事很烦。

AI 人话协议不是一本术语词典。它把 **AI 能力翻成现实麻烦**，再把 **AI 的执行过程翻回人的决定**。它要解决两次掉队：用户不知道怎样开始，以及开始以后因为看不懂而失去控制。

最高级的白话翻译，不一定是把术语解释得更长，而是让不必要的术语消失；解释产生的新陌生词越多，认知债务越高。最终合格标准不是“用户听懂一句话”，而是“用户知道接下来能做什么、要不要同意、可能失去什么”。

这份声明固定的是一套有名称、有版本、有规则、有测试和参考实现的方法，而不是试图垄断“AI 应该说人话”这句话。
